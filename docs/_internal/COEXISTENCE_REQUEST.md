## Body (plain text, ready to copy)

```
Hi Andrew,

I'm a hobbyist who builds open-source hardware. I've been working on a
project called "Wattplot" (lowercase "p") — a solar canopy that mounts
on a raised garden bed, with an ESP32 controller that tilts the panel
to follow the sun and folds it down for wind or rain. All open-source
under MIT, no commercial sales.

I just discovered your WattPlot product (capital "P") and realized we
share a name in a closely related space — solar/renewable energy. You
started in 2006, you've been selling monitoring software for OutBack
systems for almost twenty years, and you use the ™ mark. I want to
respect that.

I'm not trying to confuse anyone, and I'm not selling a competing
product. My project is a physical planter; yours is monitoring software.
Two different things, two different audiences.

My ask: are you comfortable with coexistence? Specifically, I'd
continue using "Wattplot" for the open-source hardware project at
github.com/mokahlo/wattplot and on the documentation site
mokahlo.github.io/wattplot/, clearly identifying it as an independent
open-source effort. I'd keep the WattPlot name out of any monitoring or
SaaS context where you operate.

If coexistence isn't OK, no problem — I'll rename the project before
it has any users. I'd just rather ask than guess.

Either way, thanks for building WattPlot. It's a useful tool.

— [your name]
[your GitHub handle]
github.com/mokahlo/wattplot
```

## Tone notes

- **No legalese.** Andrew is a one-person operation, not a corporation
  with a legal department. A formal "cease and desist response" framing
  would put him on the defensive.
- **No threat of "I'll just rename" as a chip.** The "I'll rename if
  you say no" line is honest, not bluster. Means it.
- **Acknowledge his seniority.** He was here 20 years before me. The
  project can and should defer.
- **Don't ask for permission to use the name in writing.** Asking for
  permission implies he has authority to grant it; he may not, and you
  don't want to put him in that position. The right ask is "are you
  comfortable with coexistence" — a yes/no on whether he'd object,
  not a license grant.

## What to do with the reply

- **"Yes, that's fine"** (or silence) — keep the name, keep the
  defensive disclaimer in `disclaimers.html` §3, add a one-line
  acknowledgment at the bottom of the trademark section crediting
  the coexistence.
- **"No, please rename"** — execute [`RENAME_PLAN.md`](RENAME_PLAN.md).
  Don't argue. The cost of renaming now is hours; the cost of a
  contested dispute later is months.
- **"I need to think about it"** — give him a reasonable window
  (2-3 weeks), then follow up once. Don't pester.
- **No reply after 2-3 weeks** — treat as soft yes, but have the
  rename plan ready in case a C&D arrives later.
