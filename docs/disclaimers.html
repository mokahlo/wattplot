1 · What "open source" means here

The Wattplot project — firmware, schematics, PCB design, 3D models, documentation, and this website — is released as open source. That means the source files are public, the design is reproducible, and you can read, modify, and redistribute them.

What you can do with it

  • Read every line of code and every wire on the schematic.
  • Build one for yourself, your family, your school, your community garden.
  • Modify it for your climate, your panel, your bed size.
  • Share your modifications with others (open-source the changes too if you can).
  • Manufacture and sell units, locally or beyond, under the terms below.

What "open source" does not mean

  • It does not mean anyone else is responsible if it breaks, burns, shocks, or underperforms.
  • It does not mean the design is certified to any electrical, structural, or safety standard.
  • It does not mean you can ignore local building codes, electrical codes, or HOA rules.
  • It does not mean the "Wattplot" name belongs to you (see §3 below).

5 · Electrical-system danger — read this twice

A solar canopy on a raised bed is a real electrical system, not a weekend toy. Treat every wire, every terminal, and every battery cell as if it can kill you, because it can.

Specific hazards in this build

  • Solar panels generate voltage whenever light hits them. You cannot turn them off. Cover them with an opaque tarp before touching the back of the panel or its wiring.
  • Lithium (LiFePO4) batteries store enough energy to deliver a fatal current through a ring of steel, or to start a fire if shorted, overcharged, punctured, or heated. Never work on a battery with jewelry on your hands.
  • 12 V DC at high current can weld metal, melt insulation, and start fires just as readily as mains AC. A 100 Ah LiFePO4 bank can push 500+ A through a low-resistance short — enough to set a wrench on fire.
  • The DRV8871 H-bridge used in the actuator path can fail short, applying full battery voltage to a stalled motor — the motor can overheat and ignite its housing within minutes.
  • Wind can move a 35°-tilted panel with enough force to injure a hand or a back. Lock or remove the panel before maintenance in any wind.

Non-negotiable safety practices

  1. Disconnect the battery AND cover the panel with an opaque tarp before doing any wiring work. Both. Always.
  2. Install an appropriately rated fuse or DC breaker within 6 inches of every battery terminal.
  3. Use wire gauge rated for the actual current — undersized wire is a fire starter.
  4. Use a charge controller matched to your panel and battery chemistry. Do not bypass it. The schematic uses a Sunapex 10A MPPT for the mini build; the full-size 620W build needs a Victron SmartSolar 75/15 or equivalent.
  5. If a battery is ever hot, swollen, or venting — evacuate the area and call your local fire department. LiFePO4 venting is less dramatic than LiPo, but the off-gas is flammable and toxic.
  6. Have a Class ABC fire extinguisher within reach when working on any battery system.

This is not an exhaustive list. Local electrical codes (NEC in the US, IEC in the EU, AS/NZS in Australia/NZ) take precedence over anything in this project. When in doubt, hire a licensed electrician.
